# How the rankings work

Not all courses are equal. A 16:00 on a flat, fast course isn't as good as a
16:00 on a hilly course in the wind. So we don't just rank by fastest time. We
adjust every time for how fast the course was.

How do we adjust for course?

---

## Week 1: Mobridge

| Runner | Time | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Jonathan | 16:00 | 16:00 |
| Gabe | 16:30 | 16:30 |
| Daniel | 17:00 | 17:00 |

With one meet there's nothing to compare against, so ratings are just times.

---

## Week 2: Custer and Rapid City

**Custer:** Gabe ran 17:20 (his usual is 16:30) and Daniel ran 17:51 (usual
17:00). Both ran **5% slower**, so Custer is **5% slow**. Based on these two runners the **whole** race is adjusted 5% faster.

**Rapid City:** Jonathan ran 15:31 (usual 16:00), **3% faster**, so Rapid City is **3% fast**. So based soley on Jonathan the **whole** race is adjusted 3% slower.

Lukas and Cody have their first race of the season, at Custer and Rapid City, respectfully. Lukas's time is adjusted based on Jonathan's performance. Cody's time is adjusted based on Gabe and Daniels performance.

| Runner | Raw time | Course | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lukas | 17:00 at Custer | 5% slow | **16:11** |
| Cody | 15:50 at Rapid City | 3% fast | **16:20** |

Ranked by PR, Cody (15:50) would be far ahead of Lukas (17:00). Adjusted, Lukas is ahead.

> ⚠️ **Possible weakness: a meet measured through one runner.** Rapid City's
> speed rests only on Jonathan. If he had an off day there, Rapid would look
> slow and Cody's rating would be wrong too. It corrects itself once Cody races
> somewhere else. In real 2025 data every meet had at least 5 linking runners,
> and most had 10 or more.

---

## Week 3: Huron and Sioux Valley

Gabe has a bad day at Huron: 16:55, which ends up being adjusted to 16:39 based on other's performance. His rating is the average of his adjusted races (16:30, 16:30, 16:39), so it's **16:33**. One bad race only nudges it.

> ⚠️ **Possible weakness: one bad day moves the course too.** Gabe's bad race
> also made Huron look a little slower than it really was (1.6% instead of
> about 1%). That shifts everyone else's Huron times slightly. In a big meet it
> gets diluted; in a small one it matters more.

---

## Week 7: after Conference and Region

| Meet | Speed |
|---|---|
| Custer | 5% slow |
| Conference | 2% slow |
| Huron | 1% slow |
| **Mobridge** | **0% (middle meet = baseline)** |
| Sioux Valley | 1% fast |
| Region | 2% fast |
| Rapid City | 3% fast |

| Rank | Runner | Rating | PR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jonathan | 16:00 | 15:31 |
| 2 | Lukas | 16:11 | 15:52 |
| 3 | Cody | 16:20 | 15:50 |
| 4 | Gabe | 16:33 | 16:10 |
| 5 | Daniel | 16:57 | 16:25 |

Cody's 15:50 PR looks better than Lukas's 15:52, but Cody's came on the fastest
course. Lukas is faster week to week.

> ⚠️ **Possible weakness: improving runners.** Daniel's Region race adjusts to
> 16:48, his best of the season. But his rating averages it with his early
> races, so he sits at 16:57. A runner who is peaking gets credit slowly.

> ⚠️ **Possible weakness: "fast course" or "fit runners"?** Everyone is fitter
> by Region, so Region looks fast partly because runners improved, not only
> because of the course. The data can't separate the two. For the rankings it
> mostly cancels, since everyone improves at about the same rate.
